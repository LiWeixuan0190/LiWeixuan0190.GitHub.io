Large-eddy Simulation with Wall Model in Lattice Boltzmann Method for Turbulent Channel Flow

View PDF of Undergraduate Thesis

Turbulent flows are essential in various engineering applications. Accurately predicting these flows is key to optimizing engineering systems design and performance. This research focuses on simulating turbulent channel flow using a combination of Large-eddy Simulation (LES) and wall models and the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM).

I developed a FORTRAN90 module to implement the Smagorinsky sub-grid scale model and the Musker wall model into the customized direct numerical simulation (DNS) code package. LES captures large-scale turbulent structures and models smaller scales, while the wall model provides accurate near-wall flow behavior, reducing grid resolution requirements.

The LES method captured streamwise velocity profiles that matched well with DNS and published data, accurately resolving both viscous sublayer and logarithmic layer flows.

Reynolds stress profiles and root-mean-square velocity distributions also aligned with DNS data, confirming the model’s ability to replicate essential turbulence characteristics. The near-wall velocity gradient, modeled via the Musker wall function, also matched the DNS data without requiring a high-resolution grid.

This research successfully validated our LES implementation through comprehensive comparisons of key turbulent statistics with DNS data. The results demonstrated great agreement while improving computational efficiency. The developed framework not only advanced our understanding of turbulent flows but also established a foundation for future urban canopy flow simulations, demonstrating the potential of combining LBM with wall-modeled LES for complex flow problems.

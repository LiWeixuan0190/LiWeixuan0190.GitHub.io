Cylinder Flow with the Immersed Boundary Method

Course: Computational Fluid Dynamics

Instructor: Rajat Mittal

View PDF of Final Report

Flow around a cylinder is a classic fluid dynamics problem that reveals vortex shedding, wake formation, and flow separation. This study uses the Immersed Boundary Method (IBM) within a fractional-step solver to simulate these flows without the need for complex body-fitted grids. The cylinder boundary is embedded in a Cartesian grid using a binary matrix approach that adjusts velocity and pressure stencils near the boundary. This approach simplifies mesh generation and ensures accurate modeling of fluid-solid interactions.

Simulations are performed at Reynolds numbers of 150, 300, and 1000. Lower Reynolds numbers exhibit steady wake patterns, while higher ones show periodic vortex shedding with more intense turbulence. The calculated drag coefficients and Strouhal numbers match benchmark data, confirming the reliability of the numerical procedure. Grid refinement studies indicate that finer grids and larger domain sizes improve accuracy but increase computational cost.

By comparing results with established references, the IBM solver shows robust performance in capturing both laminar and unsteady flow features. The combination of the Adams-Bashforth scheme for convection and the Crank-Nicolson scheme for diffusion yields stable time advancement. The fractional-step approach ensures incompressibility by maintaining a divergence-free velocity field.

The study also employs the Immersed Boundary Method to simulate flow around an ellipsoid, revealing asymmetric wake formation and varied vortex shedding compared to a cylinder. Results at Reynolds numbers 150, 300, and 1000 show distinct drag coefficients and Strouhal numbers, validating the solver’s ability to handle complex, non-circular geometries effectively.

References:

[1] Qu, L., Norberg, C., Davidson, L., Peng, S. H., & Wang, F. (2013). Quantitative numerical analysis of flow past a circular cylinder at Reynolds number between 50 and 200. Journal of Fluids and Structures, 39, 347-370.

[2] Franke, R., Rodi, W., & Schönung, B. (1990). Numerical calculation of laminar vortex-shedding flow past cylinders. Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics, 35, 237-257.

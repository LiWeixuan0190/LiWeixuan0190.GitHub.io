Computational modeling of bariatric surgery and multiphase flow simulation

This research investigated the biomechanical effects of laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG) on stomach. LSG is a bariatric surgery which reduces the stomach volume by cutting a large portion of fundus to facilitate weight loss. The study employed computational modeling and multiphase flow simulations to analyze how this surgery affects gastric functions, particularly emptying rates and mixing efficiency. The research developed three different potential postoperative stomach models with varying geometries and motility patterns to represent different surgical outcomes. This study utilizes the in-house computational fluid dynamics solver ViCar3D, which implements advanced numerical methods including immersed boundary and volume-of-fluid (VOF) approaches written in FORTRAN90. The study revealed two key findings: first, the reduced stomach volume following surgery results in significantly accelerated gastric emptying rates; second, the impaired gastric motility leads to reduced mixing efficiency, which could impact nutrient absorption and digestion quality. The research uncovered the fundamental mechanisms driving these changes, particularly the altered intragastric pressure distributions and modified patterns of antral contraction waves in post-operative conditions. These findings, presented at the 2024 APS Division of Fluid Dynamics and submitted to the Journal of Biomechanical Engineering, demonstrate the potential of computational fluid dynamics in enhancing surgical planning and improving patient outcomes.

Large-eddy Simulation with Wall Model in Lattice Boltzmann Method for Turbulent Channel Flow

Using a combination of LES and wall modeling within the Lattice Boltzmann framework, we simulate turbulent channel flows efficiently. This approach reduces computational costs while maintaining accuracy, essential for understanding complex turbulent phenomena.
